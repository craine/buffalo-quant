How to read a skater card

Points are goals plus assists, the big number. The rank compares his points per game with every NHL forward or defenseman who has played 8 or more games this season (401 forwards, 208 defensemen). The average forward in that group scores 0.55 points a game; the average defenseman, 0.37.

Chances created is what his own shots were worth in goals. Every shot that reaches the net or misses it has a chance of going in, based on where it came from and what kind of shot it was; add them up and you get his chances. The average forward creates 0.22 a game. Finishing is his goals on those shots minus his chances: positive means he's scored more than the chances were worth. Over a season, finishing bounces around much more than chances do, so a big plus or minus early on often fades.

On the back: power-play goals (scored with more skaters on the ice than the other team), shots, close-in and rebound shots, faceoffs (shown once he's taken 50), and hits, blocked shots, takeaways and giveaways. Those last four are recorded by each rink's scorers, and some rinks count more generously than others.

What's not here yet: ice time. Our feed doesn't include shift data yet, so nothing on these cards is per minute or "while he's on the ice." That's next.
