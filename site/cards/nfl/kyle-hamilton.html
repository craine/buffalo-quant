How to read an NFL card

The big number is the stat that matters most for the position: passing yards for quarterbacks, yards from scrimmage (rushing plus receiving) for running backs, receiving yards for receivers and tight ends, pressures on the quarterback for pass rushers, tackles for linebackers, and passes defended for defensive backs. The rank compares him per game with everyone at his position who has played at least 4 games.

Quarterbacks are ranked on points added per dropback. Every play changes how many points a team can expect to score on that drive, based on down, distance and field position. A 15-yard completion on 3rd and 10 adds a lot; a sack loses some. Add that up over every dropback (passes, sacks and scrambles) and divide: the league average this season is +0.05. Quarterbacks need 100 dropbacks to be ranked.

Pass rushers include defensive linemen and the edge rushers listed as linebackers: a linebacker with at least one pressure and fewer than five tackles a game is carded with the pass rushers. Pressures (sacks, hits and hurries on the quarterback) come from Pro Football Reference's game charting.

Offensive linemen get playing time and penalties only: the public data doesn't credit individual linemen for blocks or sacks allowed.

The source: nflverse's weekly player stats, snap counts and play-by-play, and Pro Football Reference charting, regular season only, through Week 9. Stats are season totals; a player who changed teams sits with the team he played for most recently. Cards marked small sample have fewer than 4 games (or 100 dropbacks for a quarterback).
