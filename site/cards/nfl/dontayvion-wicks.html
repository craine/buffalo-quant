How to read an NFL card

The big number is the stat that matters most for the position: passing yards for quarterbacks, yards from scrimmage (rushing plus receiving) for running backs, receiving yards for receivers and tight ends, pressures on the quarterback for pass rushers, tackles for linebackers and safeties, and passes defended (interceptions included) for cornerbacks. The rank compares him per game with everyone at his position who has played at least 4 games. Games include games he played only on special teams.

Quarterbacks are ranked on points added per dropback. Every play changes how many points a team can expect to score on that drive, based on down, distance and field position. A 15-yard completion on 3rd and 10 adds a lot; a sack loses some. Add that up over every dropback (passes, sacks and scrambles) and divide: across every dropback in the league this season it's +0.05. Quarterbacks need 100 dropbacks to be ranked.

Pass rushers are defensive linemen plus the linebackers who mainly rush the passer: at least 1 pressure and fewer than 6 tackles a game. Pressures (sacks, hits and hurries on the quarterback) come from Pro Football Reference's game charting. Sacks and tackles are the official counts, so they can differ a little from the charting.

Offensive linemen get playing time and penalties only: the public data doesn't credit individual linemen for blocks or sacks allowed. Fullbacks aren't ranked.

The source: nflverse's weekly player stats, snap counts and play-by-play, and Pro Football Reference charting, regular season only. Numbers are season totals. The team shown is the one he last played for; it doesn't track trades or releases after his last game. Cards marked small sample have fewer than 4 games (or 100 dropbacks for a quarterback).
