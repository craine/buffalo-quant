How to read an NFL card

The big number is the stat that matters most for the position: passing yards for quarterbacks, yards from scrimmage (rushing plus receiving) for running backs, receiving yards for receivers and tight ends, pressures on the quarterback for pass rushers, tackles for linebackers and safeties, passes defended (interceptions included) for cornerbacks, field goals for kickers and punts for punters. The rank compares him per game with everyone at his position who has played at least 4 games (quarterbacks, kickers and punters are ranked on rates instead; see below). Games include games he played only on special teams.

Quarterbacks are ranked on points added per dropback. Every play changes how many points a team can expect to score on that drive, based on down, distance and field position. A 15-yard completion on 3rd and 10 adds a lot; a sack loses some. Add that up over every dropback (passes, sacks and scrambles) and divide: across every dropback in the league this season it's +0.05. Quarterbacks need 100 dropbacks to be ranked.

Pass rushers are defensive linemen plus the linebackers who mainly rush the passer: at least 1 pressure and fewer than 6 tackles a game. Pressures (sacks, hits and hurries on the quarterback) come from Pro Football Reference's game charting. Sacks and tackles are the official counts, so they can differ a little from the charting.

Offensive linemen get playing time and penalties only: the public data doesn't credit individual linemen for blocks or sacks allowed. Fullbacks aren't ranked.

Kickers are ranked on the share of field goals made (at least 10 tries); punters on net average, the yards a punt gains after the return and touchbacks (at least 15 punts). Long snappers and players who play mostly on special teams aren't ranked; their big number is special-teams snaps, or return yards for returners.

Players who haven't played this season get a card if they're on a roster: the active roster, the practice squad or the reserve list (injured reserve and similar), as of Week 10. Their big number is their NFL games since 2018.
