## 1. The hook

**We're telling fans our own number is too high.** "Our model says 57%. Here's why we think it's wrong" is rare, honest, and
exactly the brand (receipts, no hype). It's already in the headline's second half and the first paragraph, and the hero chart
(sabres-chi-b2b.png) shows it. Nothing stronger is buried. The weaker half of the hook is "the NHL's weakest team by chances";
it's true, but after an 8-3 home loss fans won't be in a mood to hear Chicago is easy.

## 2. Headline and dek

Current headline works: it gives the reason to click (Chicago is bad) and the twist (tired legs). Alternatives, ranked:

1. **Our model says Sabres 57%. Here's why we'd read it as too high.** (Leads with the twist; the story says "Read our 57% as too high.")
2. **Tired road teams have won 38% of the time. Tonight, that's Buffalo.** (Strongest scroll-stopper; story: 38% of 643 games, Buffalo played last night, Chicago rested since Nov. 7.)
3. Keep current headline as is.

Dek is fine. Optional tightening: end on "...tired road teams have won 38%, not the 46% our model gave them." Both numbers are in the story.

## 3. Shareability

**Share sabres-chi-b2b.png.** One idea, four seasons, every season the same direction; it reads with zero context and isn't
Buffalo-specific, so non-Sabres hockey accounts can share it too.

Out-of-context risks on that chart: none serious. It doesn't say "Sabres" anywhere, so a screenshot can't be read as a
claim about Buffalo's chances. Keep it that way: never caption it as "Buffalo's chance tonight."

Second choice, sabres-chi-chances.png: clear, but "goals they were worth" on the axis needs the story to make sense, and the
inverted y-axis can confuse a fan seeing it cold. The model-test chart is for the stat crowd, not social.

### Reddit, r/sabres (image: sabres-chi-b2b.png)

**Title:** Road teams on the second night of a back-to-back vs. a rested home team have won 38% since 2023-24. That's us tonight.

Looked at every regular-season game since 2023-24. Road teams playing for the second night in a row, against a home team that had at least a day off, won 38% of 643 games. When both teams were rested, road teams won 47% (3,099 games). It was lower in every season.

Tonight fits exactly: we played Tampa last night, Chicago hasn't played since Nov. 7, and both our goalies played in last night's game.

The good news is the opponent. Chicago creates the fewest chances in the league and allows the 2nd-most. Our chances-based model has us at 57%, but it doesn't account for rest, and in games like tonight's it gave tired road teams 46% when they actually won 38%. So I'd read that 57% as too high.

Where the drop comes from is less clear: tired road teams still got about 47% of the chances in those games. Full method and charts: buffaloquant.com/analysis/sabres-blackhawks-pregame-nov-12.html

## 6. Risks

- **57% next to 38% reads like "Buffalo's real chance is 38%."** It isn't. The 38% is all tired road teams; the story never gives Buffalo an adjusted number. Never post "Buffalo ~49%" or any adjusted figure; never write "57% is really 38%." Keep "read our 57% as too high," the story's wording.
- **"Our number is too high" can sound like a betting tip** (a fade, an overvalued side). Never use value, edge, overrated, fade or anything about lines. Frame it as honesty about the model, not advice.
- **Tone after an 8-3 home loss.** Don't joke about last night, and don't sell Chicago as an easy win; "the NHL's weakest team by chances" can read as gloating and as a jinx. Lead with the back-to-back, not with Chicago being bad.
- **Goalies.** The story published without confirmed starters. Don't name tonight's starter in posts unless it's confirmed, and the story's goalie numbers are small samples.
- **Montreal, Oct. 13** (Buffalo's only other second night, a 4-3 shootout loss) is one game. Don't present it as evidence.
- **"Weakest team" vs. record.** Keep it to "fewest chances"; the story ranks chances, not standings. Chicago fans will push back on the headline wording.
- **Timing.** Dead at 8:00 p.m. ET. The X post is the priority if there's time for only one; Reddit is next (r/sabres game threads go up before puck drop).
