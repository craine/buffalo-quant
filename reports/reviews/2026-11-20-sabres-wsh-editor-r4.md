# Editor review, round 4 (independent): reports/2026-11-20-sabres-wsh.md

**One sentence a host would say:** "The Sabres snapped a five-game skid by scoring four in the first period in Washington, even though
the Capitals had about twice the chances."

**Verdict: fail.** The story reads well and the structure is right. Four sentences say something untrue or easy to misread, and one
chart title contradicts its own bars.

## Must fix

1. **Lede: "After five games of cold shooting"** isn't true of every game. In the last skid game (PHI) Buffalo scored more than its
   chances, and the finishing chart shows that red bar above zero.
   "This time the puck went in. After five games of cold shooting, it was Buffalo's second-best shooting night of the season."
   -> "This time the puck went in. After a cold-shooting skid, it was Buffalo's second-best shooting night of the season."
2. **Finishing chart title** has the same problem, and a screenshot reader sees the red bar above zero.
   "After a skid of cold shooting, the Sabres' second-best shooting night"
   -> "Thursday was the Sabres' second-best shooting night of the season"
3. **"and tonight it did"** says cold shooting has evened out. One game doesn't do that: they're still behind their chances over the skid
   plus tonight and over the season.
   "Our GM story said cold shooting tends to even out, and tonight it did."
   -> "Our GM story said cold shooting tends to even out. For one night, at least, the puck went in."
4. **Benson line** reads as if his two goals came on chances worth 7.9. It also takes a second read.
   "Two of his three goals this season came with a goalie in net, on chances worth 7.9."
   -> "Not counting an empty-netter, he has 2 goals this season on shots worth about 7.9 goals."
5. **Lede: "and Alex Tuch scored against his old team"** sits in a list of Sabres scorers, so a casual fan hears Tuch as a Sabre who
   scored for Buffalo.
   "Zach Benson scored, Tage Thompson got his 14th, and Alex Tuch scored against his old team."
   -> "Zach Benson scored, Tage Thompson got his 14th, and former Sabre Alex Tuch scored for Washington."

## Optional polish

  moment. -> "In the third period Washington took over: its chances were worth 2.2 goals to Buffalo's 0.4, and it scored twice before
  Jack Quinn's empty-netter."
- **"Mostly, yes, and that's not a knock."** A knock on whom? -> "Mostly, yes. Every team needs nights like this."
  (or cut "and that's not a knock").
- **Repeated "9 shots worth 1.2 goals"**: it's in "Was it luck?" and again in "The penalty kill". Cut it from the luck section. End that
  paragraph at "...What it said to fix first was the penalty kill; more on that below." Then drop "Overall, Washington's chances were worth
  about twice Buffalo's." The lede already says it.
- **"tonight" vs. "Thursday"**: the story is dated Nov. 20 and the chart says "Thursday", but the body says "tonight" six times. Pick one.
  For a next-morning story, "Thursday" works everywhere.
- **Finishing chart label "Thursday"** doesn't stand alone in a screenshot. Use "Nov. 19 at WSH" or put the date in the subtitle.
- **"2nd-best"** in the body vs. "second-best" in the lede and chart. Use "second-best".
- **Card dek "Washington had twice the chances"** reads as twice as many shots (it was 53 to 23). -> "Washington's chances were worth
  twice Buffalo's."
- **Predictive**: "What's next" is only the schedule. One forward-looking line would help, e.g. whether Florida and Tampa Bay test the
  penalty kill. Only use numbers the generator computes.
